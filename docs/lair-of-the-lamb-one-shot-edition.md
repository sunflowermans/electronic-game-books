# The Pitch

In the frigid darkness, your pulse quickens to the eerie echoes of a dragging, prodigious bulk. Survive the relentless pursuit of a monstrous abomination in this heart-pounding, highly lethal escape from its lair. Players control multiple 0-level characters as they attempt to flee their grim fates. Play smart, use your resources wisely, and there may yet be hope of escape for a lucky few.

# Adventure Overview

The party starts with 12 to 15 level 0 peasants, randomly generated. It is expected that most will not survive the dungeon.

The characters do not know it, but they are beneath [The White Temple](#the-white-temple), where they have been offered as sacrifices to [The Lamb](#the-lamb). They are expected to be leisurely devoured over several days.

There's one loop in the dungeon, which is very valuable when the party is trying to escape a big, slow foe. It's an imperfect loop, since [12 MOUTH MURAL](#12-mouth-mural) can be collapsed and [The Lamb](#the-lamb) will eventually learn to block off the [secret tunnel](#10-bone-pile).

Deaths are expected, but that's why there are so many characters. The players will learn from their deaths. And yes, the lamb is too difficult to easily kill, but the fact that it stops to eat will avoid a TPK. If a player runs out of living characters, ask for a volunteer to offer up one of their extras for that player to control.

Light, ropes, and weapons are all precious in this scenario. Don't allow the players to sidestep these constraints. Follow torch durations and make [improvised items](#improvised-items) unreliable.

The expected (but not required) flow is:

- The party initially flees.
- The party lures [The Lamb](#the-lamb) away from [9 FOUNTAIN](#9-fountain) and then drinks water.
- The party follows the main path to end up at [13 CITY MURAL](#13-city-mural). The bashed in door reaffirms that they can and should be bashing in [locked doors](#doors).
- The party escapes via [21 HEAVY DOORS](#21-heavy-doors).

# A Message For Players

{: .note-title .callout }

> Suggestion
>
> Use this script if you are playing with new people or just in a situation where your players are not familiar with this play-style or funnels in general.  

You control multiple characters and most of them will die. Your goal is to escape with as many characters as you can. 

Individually your characters are just level 0 peasants but together, the group can accomplish great things. If they don't work together, your characters will likely perish. A fair fight is not the best way to survive. Your characters are bad at fighting and one hit will kill them. Think smart about how to approach situations and how the environment might help you accomplish your goal.

Time is an important resource. Time burns out torches which provide light. I roll for random encounters with monsters to keep the pressure on your characters. The more time spent in the dungeon, the more opportunities there are for your characters to die.

This game relies on a certain level of trust in the game master. My goal is not to kill your characters. I'm rooting for them. However, I am also responsible for running the monsters and some of them want to eat you. My goal is to act as a neutral arbiter.

In general, don't worry about the rules, just tell me what you want to try to do, and we can work it out together.

Any questions?

# Core Rules

## Dont Worry About It

You are the referee and you don't have to teach the rules to the players. You do have to tell them the odds—what dice they are rolling and the result they need to get. You should tell the players that you are periodically rolling for [random encounters](#raandom-encounters) and let them know when their torches start guttering.

Run your game however is easiest for you. There's enough to do already. Offload turn keeping and light duration to the players if you think that'll be easier.

## Player Dice Rolls

If actions cannot be resolved through discussion, the player rolls a d20 and attempts to meet or exceed a target number. Unless otherwise noted, the target number is **16**—terrible odds. Some rolls will allow for modifiers to be added to the d20 roll, but most will not.

### Bonuses, Penalties, and Automatic Success

When the players have a really good plan, there is no need to roll the dice. The plan succeeds.

Bonuses or Penalties of **+-1 or +-2** may be added to player rolls if the situation calls for it, like multiple characters assisting to lift a gate.

#### Advantage and Disadvantage

When the circumstances of a player roll are considerably good or bad, the referee may decide that the roll is made at Advantage or Disadvantage.

Players roll **2d20 and take the higher or lower die result**, respectively.

Having both advantage and disadvantage negates both effects, regardless of how many.

#### Dehydration

Characters begin dehydrated and have [disadvantage](#advantage-and-disadvantage) on all rolls until they drink water. Without water, everyone will be dead in 24 hours.

### Die of fate

If something is not obvious and not covered by another dice roll, the referee may call for an X-in-6 chance. A d6 is rolled by the player. A result of **X or less** indicates a success.

The exact odds of the roll should be discussed between the referee and players. Default to **3-in-6**.

## Inventory

Characters start naked. Players need to track what items they are carrying and how they are carrying them.

## Improvised Items

Adjudicate improvised item ideas as needed. Make sure they are unreliable and remember to tell the players the odds associated with their improvised item. A really good idea could make an improvised item better or hit harder. Here are some examples of improvised items: 

**Improvised weapons** (-1 HP) break on [attack rolls](#attacking) of 1. (bricks, table legs, torches, ect.)

**Improvised burlap ropes** break on 3-in-6 when used.

**Improvised torches** burn erratically. There's a 3-in-6 chance they burn out each [dungeon turn](#dungeon-turns). Improvised torches take a full [dungeon turn](#dungeon-turns) to light.

## Combat

### Rounds

Combat is broken into abstracted time called **rounds**. This is about 10 seconds.

### Attacking

Players determine if their character [hits](#hit-points) a monster by rolling a d20 with a target number equal to the **monster's Guard**.

### Defending

Players determine if their character is [hit](#hit-points) by an attacking monster by rolling a d20 with a target number of the **monster's Power**.

Armored characters may add their total Defense to the d20 roll.

### Hit Points

When a creature is hit, it loses Hit Points. When all of it's Hit Points are lost, the creature is dead. 

Characters have one Hit Point.

Attacks remove a fixed number of Hit Points from their target with each successful [attack roll](#attacking).

* [**Improvised weapons**](#improvised-items): -1 HP.

* **Regular weapons**: -2 HP.

* **Magic weapons**: -3 HP.

### Spells

Monsters may cast each of their spells once per day.

### Actions (combat)

In general, characters can perform **one combat action per round and move**. Here are some examples of combat actions:

* Attack
* Cast a spell
* Run / Flee
* Light a torch
* Tie a knot or lasso
* Take a drink

### Movement

Movement in combat is abstracted. Humans move at "normal speed." It's about 40' per [round](#round). 

### Initiative

One player rolls a d6 to determine which side acts first, the characters or the monsters. **4-6** indicates the players go first, **1-3** indicates the monsters act first.

### Sequence of play (combat)

1. The players state what actions their characters will attempt.
2. One player rolls **[initiative](#initiative)**.
3. The side that wins initiative resolves their [**actions**](#actions).
4. The other side resolves their [**actions**](#actions).

# Dungeon Crawling

## Light

The dungeon is pitch black. Fumbling around in the dark is risky and takes time. [Player dice rolls](#player-dice-rolls) in the dark are at [disadvantage](#advantage-and-disadvantage).

| Light Source | Illumination Range | Duration |
| :------------ | :------------------ | :-----------|
| Torch | 30'       | 1 hour  |
| Lantern (requires oil) | 30'       | 4 hours |
| Candle | 10'       | 1 hour  |

## Dungeon Turns

Out of combat, time is abstracted into dungeon turns. They are about 10 minutes of in-game time.

## Actions (dungeon crawling)

Dungeon crawling actions usually take one dungeon turn. Here are some examples of dungeon crawling actions:

- Searching a room or body.
- Prying a torch sconce off the wall.
- Passing through a room for the first time.

Yes, this is slow. It is assumed that the party is crawling at dungeon speed. This means that everyone is creeping along quietly, talking in a whisper, noticing all the details of the rooms they are in, feeling out every step they take, and listening to all the ambient noises that they can.

## Sequence of play (dungeon crawling)

1. If indicated, light source(s) go out.
2. If indicated, the referee checks for [random encounters](#random-encounters).
3. Each player tells the referee what actions their characters attempt.
4. The referee resolves actions (prompting players for rolls if needed) and presents encounters if indicated.
5. The referee marks the [dungeon turn](#dungeon-turns) as ended.

## Doors

It is always assumed that characters listen at doors before they open them since they are going at dungeon speed.

**Wooden doors** can be kicked in, but it makes noise (which triggers a [random encounter](#random-encounters) roll). If the kicker succeeds on a [d20 roll](#player-dice-rolls), they can do it in a negligible amount of time. Otherwise it takes one [dungeon turn](#dungeon-turns).

**Metal doors** cannot be kicked in.

# Random Encounters

Check for encounters **once every three [dungeon turns](#dungeon-turns)** or if the characters make **significant noise**, such as from breaking down a [door](#doors).

Roll a d6. 

* On a 1, [The Lamb](#the-lamb) is encountered and attacks the characters. If the characters have no light, [The lamb](#the-lamb) has a 3-in-6 chance of surprising them, gaining a free attack before [initiative](#initiative) is rolled.
* On a 2, [The Lamb](#the-lamb) is encountered, but it is unaware of the characters' presence. The lamb is in an adjacent room, snuffling and grunting. After one [dungeon turn](#dungeon-turns), it will go in a random direction. You do not need to track it's position at all times. If [Room 12 MOUTH MURAL](#12-mouth-mural) is collapsed, [The Lamb](#the-lamb) will be trapped on the north side of the dungeon, unless you know for a fact that it is to the south.

If characters triggered the encounter by making **noise**, the lamb attacks on a 1 or a 2.


# The Lamb

[![luke-just-lamb-2](/assets/images/lair-of-the-lamb-one-shot-edition/luke-just-lamb-3.png)](/assets/images/lair-of-the-lamb-one-shot-edition/luke-just-lamb-3.png)

The Lamb is one of the secrets that [The White Temple](#the-white-temple) keeps. They call it The Lamb because it was born of a sheep. It is a holy child of [Vandoh](#the-white-temple).

It looks like a gigantic, hairless, obese cow. It has no obvious head, its neck instead terminating in a drooping cowl of wrinkled flesh. It's actual head (usually kept
retracted) resembles a bloody sheep skull with bulging eyes and a black tongue. It extends its head when it needs to bite something. It weighs about 2500 lbs. It drags its belly as it walks. Clusters of [bulbous nodules](#lambfruit) along it's sides and underbelly sway as it moves. It reeks of blood and ammonia.

The Lamb's sight and hearing are slightly worse than the average human's. When the lamb is wounded or scared, it will bleat “Faaather! Faaather!” in increasingly loud tones. It is capable of no other speech. It is not smart, but it is clever. It learns from its mistakes and will learn better tactics with exposure.

It mostly hangs out in [8 PIT](#8-pit) and [9 FOUNTAIN](#9-fountain).

{: .monster }

> **The Lamb**
>
> **Hit Points**: 14, **Power**: 16, **Guard**: 10, **Attacks**: Bite (-2 HP), **Move**: Slow
>
> * **Surprise**: In darkness, The Lamb has a 3-in-6 chance of surprising the party, gaining an attack before [initiative](#initiative) is rolled.
> * **Feeding**: If the lamb kills someone, or if finds fresh meat (a goat), it will stop and feed for one [dungeon turn](#dungeon-turns). During this time, it is not very alert.
> * **Fleeing**: The Lamb is slower than the average human, but characters must win [initiative](#initiative) to run past it in a hallway or room. If the lamb loses track of its prey, it will continue running through 1d4 more rooms (chosen at random from available paths).

## Lambfruit

The Lamb is valued for its fruit, clusters of heavy nodules that grow along its armpits and milk lines.


Here are the types of Lambfruit (and how many of each type are currently hanging off the Lamb):

- **Nascent Lambfruit (52)**: Dark green. Consumption causes nausea, vomiting, and rampant paranoia about the future. For the rest of the adventure any failed [d20 rolls](#player-dice-rolls) by the character spends the next round [retching](#rounds).
- **Immature Lambfruit (6)**: Orange. Consumption allows you to see six seconds into the future, per the spell [precognition](#precognition).

The characters begin in [1 BOWLS](#1-bowls). They are naked and wriggling inside burlap sacks. It's pitch black.

{: .highlight }

> You wake up stiff and groggy. You don't know where you are or how you got here. It's cold.
>
> Your lips are cracked and dry—it's hard to think, you're so thirsty. You find yourself inside a tied sack, naked in the dark.
>

Select one random player's character that finds a knife.

{: .highlight }

> You feel a knife in your bag and manage to cut yourself and a few others free. You can hear more people struggling to free themselves in the dark.

Everyone should be in control of one free character now. Their other characters are still restrained in sacks.

{: .highlight }

> You hear a door creak open. Heavy, wet footsteps shuffle closer. There is the sound of wet, sucking breath. The sound of a prodigious bulk being dragged.

[The Lamb](#the-lamb) is lumbering into the room.

* **Fleeing**: [The Lamb](#the-lamb) attacks. It will take a [d20 roll](#player-dice-rolls) to find the door in the dark while under duress.

* **Hiding**: [The Lamb](#the-lamb) devours a restrained human over one [dungeon turn](#dungeon-turns).

When the Lamb finishes eating, or loses track of the party, it will head to [9 FOUNTAIN](#9-fountain) for a nice bath.

{: .monster #the-skull-of-davok }

> **The Skull of Davok**
>
> This is the remnant of a powerful demon. It resembles an elephant skull, slightly deformed.
>
> * **Released from chest**: It will attempt to absorb flesh every round until they succeed ([d20 roll](#olayer-dice-rolls) or die). It can fly about as fast as a human can walk. If the skull fails to absorb flesh, add Davok to the [Random Encounters](#random-encounters) roll (appearing on 3).
> * **Absorbing Flesh**: It regrows much of its head. (It looks nothing at all like an elephant.) This will allow it to regain something of its mind, and it will demand to be carried out of the dungeon, casting _Enthrall_ on people until it gets what it wants ([d20 roll](#player-dice-rolls) or become a thrall to Davok).
> * **While Carried**: It will appear to go to sleep while it searches the ether for the other pieces of its fragmented body.
> * **The Enthralled**: A thrall is still a playable character, but they are utterly dedicated to serving Davok and will not tolerate any word or action against their master. If pressed, they will call Davok's name.
> * **Awakening**: Davok will awake if it is touched by another, or if its name is called. In combat, it explodes (-3 HP to everything within 20'). This stuns Davok for one [dungeon turn](#dungeon-turns) while it knits itself back together.
> * **Weakness**: There is nothing in this dungeon capable of destroying it. If Davok is ever forced back into the leaden chest, he once again becomes inert.
>
> Once free from the dungeon, Davok begins collecting his missing body parts. His loyal thrall will become a warlock in his service. If they do not wish to become a warlock, they will be made to. The rest of the party can either join in service to Davok, or go their own way. If they choose the latter, the newly minted warlock becomes an allied NPC within the [city of Manderlon](#the-free-city-of-manderlon).

## 7 THRONE

A **throne** of sheep bones and unfired clay. Two **skeletons** stand beside the throne, one holds something black, the other holds something gold. 

**Skeletons**: Held together with wire (10'). One skeleton carries a black mace. The other carries a gold plated telescope (500 sp). When a skeleton is approached it kneels and outstretches its arms, offering what it is holding.

* Failure (Obesity): Gain 3 Inventory slots of fat. Right hand turns into a small lobster claw (1 hit).

A mural of a comet above a tower. A **scrimshaw sheep skull** rests on a pedestal.

* **Scrimshaw Sheep Skull**: Carvings of battles (40s)

## 12 MOUTH MURAL

The **sagging roof** is held up by a 11' metal pole. A **mural of a mouth**. 

* **Sagging Roof**: When the pole is removed, the ceiling collapses and crushes all in the room (-3 HP).

  * If the ceiling falls in, this room becomes impassible.

  * Running through during a collapse requires a [d20 roll](#d20-player-rolls) to avoid the the rubble.


* **Mural of a Mouth**: Each tooth is actually a tiny building. Several windows hold tiny, staring figures.

## 13 CITY MURAL

A mural of a city under waves and a looming giant crab. A huge **crack** runs down the length of the south wall, filled with spiderwebs. A sagging, **moldy door** barely blocks passage east.

* **Crack**: The light of day glows against the inside of this fissure. The faint breeze coming from it brings the smell of roasted chicken.

* **Moldy Door**: Badly damaged and can be quietly wrenched off its hinges.

## 14 SARCOPHAGUS

A **stone sarcophagus**. A crawl tunnel near the base of the north wall.

* **Stone Sarcophagus**: The heavy lid can be pushed aside by at least 3 people, but it makes noise ([random encounter](#random-encounter)).
  * Inside is a shriveled corpse without any hands. On its chest are a pearl ring (100s) and a black pearl ring (200s).

## 15 CRACK

Hundreds of **tiny red spiders**. You can barely fit in here. There's **sounds** of an argument between two men.

* **Tiny Red Spiders**: Several bites every second spent in the crack. They cause profuse sweating and your heart to race. 
* **Sounds**: Shouting about the quality of a chicken.
* **At the very back**: Daylight through an opening the size of a fist.
  * A chicken vendor named **Danjo** argues with a customer in the plaza beside [The White Temple](#the-white-temple). 
* **Danjo**: Extorts characters at the first opportunity. *“Why shouldn't I tell the temple guards? What's in it for me?”* Once that's taken care of, he will happily sell you mundane items at as high a cost as he can.
  * Has candles, chickens, bread, and a knife. He is willing to run across town and purchase things for you, but it'll take 1-3 hours, depending on the item.

## 16 MOLD

A **moldy shape** curled up in the center of the room. 

* **Moldy Shape**: Looks like a corpse obscured under a heavy layer of thick fungus.
  * Smells like mutton.
  * It's just mold that grew in a weirdly shaped heap. Each time the party returns to this room, the mold appears to be in a slightly different position.
  * The first person to taste the mold or sleep in this room will receive hallucinations of [Shendormu](#the-red-temple), the good god of fungus. The god will appear as a dim, fungal moon, and will answer one question honestly if the character promises to receive sacrament at his temple. 

{: .note .callout } 

> Receiving sacrament at [The Red Temple](#the-red-temple) allows a character to become a cleric of Shendormu, if they wish.

## 17 ALTAR

An **altar** holds a sheep skull amid faded flowers. A **wooden chest** sags with yellow rot.

* **Altar**: Drawer containing six white candles.
* **Wooden Chest**: Anyone opening it carelessly is exposed to plumes of yellow mold spores ([d20 roll](#player-dice-rolls) or die).
  * Contains rotten books (worthless) and a silver-plate dageurrotype (50s) that shows a dozen strangely-dressed men each buried up to their waist, facing the stars, mouth open.

## 18 SHAFT

10' heap of broken and shattered **wooden beams and debris**. Above, a **high ceiling** recedes into shadow.

**Wooden beams and debris**: Surrounded by loops of rope, all soft and rotten.

* Remains of a fallen elevator.
* Searching the pile unearths a block and tackle.

**High Ceiling**: Terminates 60' above.

* 40' up is a loop of rope that stretches another 10' connecting to [18A LEDGE](18a-ledge).

## 18A LEDGE

A 30' rope tied to an iron spike. A sealed portcullis. 10' away, on the other side, is an **armored corpse**. 

**Armored Corpse**: Cannot pass through the portcullis, but it can be pulled to the portcullis and looted:

- Sword (-2 HP)
- Bow + Arrows (-2 HP)
- Signet ring (50s)
- [Orb of Indulgence](#orb-of-indulgence) (500s)

## 19 PRIESTS

{: .note .callout}

> The wooden door to this room is locked.

Three perfectly round shallow **pools** of pink liquid are set into the floor. In each pool sleeps a squatting **old man**. Two iron spikes hold the east door shut.

* **Pools**: About a foot deep in the middle.
  * Smells like bubble gum.
  * Characters who drink from a pool are compelled to drink more and eventually lie down in it. Lasts 10 minutes before it fades from a compulsion to a lingering dissatisfaction with all of life's other pleasures.
  
* **Old men (3)**: Naked. Smiling in their sleep, occasionally twitching and emitting little groans of pleasure.
  * One has an emerald lens (400s) embedded in their forehead. 
  * Retired [Priests of Vandoh](#priest-of-vandoh), rewarded for service. They will live in the Lamb's psychotropic urine until they die, hundreds of years from now, writhing in ecstasy.
  * When touched, they will scream (verbally and psychically). This awakens all priests (including [Vandress](#vandress) in [20 POOLS](#20-pools)) who will attack. The
    urine has robbed these three priests of all language except the words “lamb”, “blasphemy”, and “magenta”.

{: .monster #priest-of-vandoh }

> **Priest of Vandoh**
>
> **Hit Points**: 2 **Guard**: 10, **Power**: 11, **Attacks**: Slap (-0 HP) or Spell
>
> * **Spells**: Range is 30'. [d20 roll](#player-dice-rolls) to negate. Each priest knows a different spell and may cast it once per day.
>   * Sleep: Target falls into a deep sleep. Noises will not wake them, but a slap or a kick will.
>   * Desiccate: Evaporates five gallons of liquid. -3 HP to one mostly liquid target, like a human.
>   * Force Teleport: Target is teleported to a random room of the dungeon.



## 20 POOLS

Four irregular **pools**, each one contains a different color liquid.

* **Pink pool**: Identical to the pink pools in [19 PRIESTS](#19-priests). 
  * Contains [Vandress](#vandress), the eldest priestess. She has lobster claws and a third eye.
* **Yellow pool**: Liquid light.
  * Shines as brightly as daylight for 3 rounds when violently splashed. Works once.
  * Can be collected into a bottle without triggering it.

- **Milky pool**: Acid (-2 HP).
- **Black pool**: Fall unconscious and vomit out a thick black smoke that's heavier than air. This smoke is your soul.
    - You see and hear normally. You move as gas.
    - The effect lasts until you re-enter your body.

The pools serve as a repository for the more exotic types of waste that the Lamb produces.

{: .monster #vandress }

> **Vandress**
>
> **Hit Points**: 4 **Guard**: 10, **Power**: 12, **Attacks**: Claw -2 HP or Spell
>
> * **Spells**
>   * Confusion: Each round, target creature must declare three potential actions that are fully distinct from each other (no shared statblock. Each monster has a number of magic dice (MD) performs one of the three actions at random.
>   * Cone of Mutilation: -3 HP to everything in a 20' cone from caster.
> * **Scheming**: Unlike her peers, Vandress retains her full mental capacity, and is smart enough to parlay if the need arises. If allowed to escape, she will find [The Lamb](#the-lamb) and lead it while hunting the party.
> * **Knows**: The clerics come down three days after a feeding to clean up.


## 21 HEAVY DOORS

A long set of steep stairs lead up to this room and a pair of barricaded **iron doors**. There's **chatter and laughter** coming from the other side of the door.

**Iron Doors**: Kept closed with a heavy bar (10') and a set of chains.

- The bar can be pulled off with the combined strength of two people. This allows the doors to open a small amount. A human could squeeze through but not [The Lamb](#the-lamb).
- The chain cannot be removed without heavy tools. It would allow the doors to open fully.

**Chatter and Laughter**: Gentlemanly-dressed decaying corpses—the ghouls Gaspar and Luntz. Gaspar carries a silver-handled cane (50s).

* Gaspar is speaking to the door as if it were the host of a party, accusing it of not sharing the best brandy with them.
* Upon hearing the party fumbling with the door, the ghouls feign friendship (poorly), intending to eat the party at the first opportunity. They speak openly of how hungry they are.
* When promised food to be given later for safe passage now, the ghouls pretend to believe characters. 
* When fed well (at least a goat's worth of meat each), the ghouls are friendly and conversant.
